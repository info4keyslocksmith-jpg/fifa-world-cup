01 · Eligibility

Stop — this decides the whole thing

A mail-in-only business cannot have a Google profile.

Google's eligibility rule is that a business must have a physical location, or meet customers face to face. Selling only through a website does not qualify. As TwinECM is written today — nationwide, ship it to us — it is not eligible.

What makes it eligible is the drop-off. The homepage already offers "mail it in or drop it off." If customers can physically hand you a module at a real address you control and staff, that is face-to-face contact and the listing is legitimate. That has to be true in practice, not just on the site.

The address rules that get people suspended

  • No PO box, no virtual office, no mailbox rental. A UPS Store box or a coworking address is the single most common cause of suspension in this category.
  • A home address is allowed, and you can hide it. Google still needs it behind the scenes to verify you.
  • Hide the address if there is no signage where you take walk-ins. Then it lists as a service-area business.
  • Service area is capped at roughly two hours' drive from your base, up to 20 areas. You cannot set "nationwide."

So be honest about what this channel is

The profile will only ever bring you local work — drop-offs and shops inside metro Atlanta. Your nationwide mail-in volume comes from Google Ads and the symptom pages, not from here. That is still worth doing: the local pack is free, it ranks for "ECM replacement near me," and a profile with real reviews makes the ads more believable. Just do not expect it to carry the business.

Atlanta is not an empty field, though. Auto Module Programming runs a mobile ECU, BCM and TCM service across Buckhead, Marietta, Alpharetta and Sandy Springs — the same metro, overlapping work. And UpFix, the one competitor advertising at scale nationally, is also a Georgia company. Neither is a reason not to list, but the local pack here has to be earned on reviews and photos, not won by showing up.

Your edge against a mobile operator is the opposite of theirs: they drive to the car, you work a bench. Say the bench part — verified before it ships, unlock included, no board damage — because that is what a mobile van cannot do in a driveway.

02 · Name

Exactly this, and nothing after it

Business name8 characters
TwinECM

Your profile name has to be your real-world business name with no descriptors attached. TwinECM ECM Cloning, TwinECM — Engine Computer Cloning Tampa, or anything with a city, a service, a tagline, a phone number or a URL in it is keyword stuffing, and it is enforced. Google blocked roughly 70 million listing edits and removed 12 million fake profiles in 2024; appeals take 30 days or more, and the listing is invisible the whole time.

The keywords belong in the description, the services and the posts, where they are allowed and where they still do the ranking work. Not in the name.

06 · Atlanta — hours, area, contact

Based in Atlanta, so the area is the metro

Fill in as

  • City: Atlanta, GA
  • Address: the real drop-off address
  • Hours: open every day, set the times
  • Website: twinecm.com, the homepage
  • Phone: see the flag below

Watch for

  • Hours must be when the phone is actually answered — "open" with no pickup is what earns one-star reviews
  • Do not set a tracking link as the website
  • Do not stretch the area past what you would really drive
  • Same address, phone and name everywhere online, character for character

The area code does not match the city

(813) is Tampa. The business is in Atlanta. This will not get the listing suspended — Google stopped weighting area codes long ago — but it is friction on exactly the customer this profile exists to win. Someone searching "ECM replacement near me" in Marietta, seeing an Atlanta address and a Florida number, hesitates.

Get a 404, 470, 678 or 770 number and forward it to the existing line. 404 reads as in-town Atlanta and is the strongest of the four. Keep the 813 as the number printed on mail-in paperwork if it is already in circulation, but the profile, the website and the ads should all show the Atlanta number, identically.

Service area — counties, not cities

Google caps the area at roughly two hours' drive and 20 entries. Counties cover more ground per entry than cities and are how Georgia is usually carved up. Nine counties covers the metro without overreaching:

That reaches Buckhead, Sandy Springs, Marietta, Alpharetta, Roswell, Decatur, Duluth, Lawrenceville, Kennesaw and Johns Creek — including the four suburbs the local competitor names. Leave the other eleven slots empty for now. A tight area ranks better than a sprawling one, and you can add Paulding, Fayette, Coweta, Rockdale, Newton, Hall or Bartow later if drop-offs actually come from there.

Do not add Athens, Macon, Chattanooga or Columbus. They are inside two hours on paper and nobody is driving a module that far when they could mail it.

One more thing to expect: the site says open every day while the service promises one business day turnaround. Someone will ask. The Q&A below answers it rather than leaving them to guess.

08 · Photos

Shoot these, in this order

Real photos of the bench outperform anything staged, and this category has almost no good imagery on Google, so it is cheap to win. Match the brand rule already set: real modules, real boxes, hands doing the work, no stock photography.

  • Logo — twin bars on the dark ground, square crop.
  • Cover — the bench with a module connected, cyan cast, wide.
  • A module with its sticker readable — this is the photo that tells a mechanic you are real, and it teaches customers where to look for the part number.
  • The bench mid-clone — harness, power supply, screen showing a read in progress.
  • A packed return box — unit bagged, labelled, install sheet on top.
  • The drop-off point — only if you have signage. Required if you list an address customers visit.
  • You, at the bench — profiles with a face convert better than anonymous ones.

10 · Reviews

Ask two days after it starts

The follow-up text already in the order flow — "Did it start?" — is the review ask. A yes is the moment to send the link, and never before.

Review request textSend after a "yes"
Glad it's running. If you have a minute, a short review helps other people find us — especially if you mention your vehicle and that it was a cloning job. [review link]

Asking them to name the vehicle and the service is the whole trick: a review that says "2014 Ram 1500, cloned my PCM, started first try" ranks the profile for that exact search and answers the next customer's question at the same time. Three phrases to hope for, from what customers already say about competitors: plugged it in and it started, communicated the whole time, turnaround as promised.

Never

Buy reviews, offer a discount for one, or set up a kiosk that filters unhappy customers out. All three are against Google's policy, all three are detectable, and in a category this small a fake-review pattern is obvious.

11 · Before you click publish

Seven things, and five of them are still open

  • A real, staffed Atlanta address you controlNot a mailbox, not a virtual office. This is the gate — nothing else matters until it exists.
  • Drop-off is genuinely offeredThe face-to-face contact that makes the listing legitimate has to be real.
  • Opening hours decidedThe site says open every day; the profile needs actual times.
  • twinecm.com registered and liveA profile pointing at a dead domain wastes the traffic it earns.
  • Name entered as TwinECM, nothing appendedThe single easiest way to lose the listing for a month.
  • An Atlanta phone number404, 470, 678 or 770, forwarded to the existing line, and used identically on the site, the profile and the ads.
  • Verification method chosenUsually postcard or video. Video verification wants the address, the bench and you in one unbroken take — worth planning before you start it.
